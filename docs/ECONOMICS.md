# Lifecycle economics

Run `gridpass-flexibility --economics examples/lifecycle-economics.json` or call the existing generic evaluation interface with operation `economics` and the same JSON object. This is an engineering comparison of supplied connection alternatives, not an investment forecast.

Each alternative specifies a complete 8,760-hour operating-year dispatch scenario, capex paid at project start, connection delay in months, annual contribution before modeled costs, annual fixed operating cost, and an explicit grid emissions factor (kg CO2e/MWh). Optional replacements are paid at the specified project year end and residual value is credited at the final year end. All values use constant EUR, with an explicit real discount rate and common analysis horizon of 1–50 years. Electricity prices must exclude separately added carbon and peak charges; generator variable costs must exclude separately supplied fuel and carbon costs. Generator fuel assumptions specify electrical efficiency, fuel EUR/MWh and fuel kg CO2e/MWh on a consistent thermal energy basis. Generator fuel budget in the dispatch scenario remains an electrical MWh limit.

The optimizer minimizes import-cap gaps before economic costs. Choose either `annual_peak_eur_mw` (one annual maximum) or `monthly_peak_eur_mw` (12 nonnegative Jan–Dec rates in EUR/MW, one maximum sampled import per month). Monthly billing assumes a non-leap year beginning January 1 and equal intervals aligned with calendar month boundaries; double billing and misaligned samples are rejected. Monthly results expose each peak, rate and charge. The same HiGHS dispatch model optimizes energy, fuel, carbon, peak and other variable costs; all components are independently reconstructed from the schedule and reconciled. Startup and online costs come from the discrete dispatch model. Grid emissions are a supplied accounting factor, not marginal emissions forecasts.

Each operating year repeats the supplied annual schedule. For project year y, service fraction is clamp((12*y-delay_months)/12,0,1). Contribution, fixed annual costs and modeled annual operating cost (including peak fee) are prorated by this fraction. Net cashflow subtracts replacement cost and adds declared residual value at the end. NPV is minus initial investment plus the sum of discounted year-end cashflows. Delay changes the available service years and the value of time-to-power without adding a second lost-revenue charge. Compare incremental NPV against a named feasible reference. A design that never enters service, violates the modeled cap or has an unsolved dispatch cannot win. If any alternative in a sensitivity fails to solve, that comparison is incomplete and no recommendation is issued.

For an alternative with monthly peak tariffs, opt in with `"seasonal_commissioning": true` and a whole-month connection delay (including sensitivity delays). Project year one starts in January. The first partial year is independently optimized on the remaining calendar samples, with the declared resource initial states and zero workload backlog. Events are clipped to the active horizon; notice cannot precede commissioning. The `commissioning` result reports feasibility, uncovered energy, active monthly costs and first service month. A failed solve blocks a recommendation; a cap violation makes the alternative ineligible. The full annual result still reports twelve reconciled monthly costs. Contribution and fixed costs still use the service-month fraction. Fuel budgets are the declared available amounts for each solve; no pre-service energy cost or inter-year reoptimization is modeled. Supply actual commissioning state and available fuel for a site study.

Supply up to eight alternatives and eight explicit sensitivities. Each sensitivity can multiply electricity, fuel, carbon and investment costs or add connection delay to all alternatives. Dispatch is reoptimized, not merely repriced. A single shared solver time budget defaults to 60 seconds (CLI allows up to 600); the local MCP limits complexity and time. A timeout is an incomplete evaluation.

The synthetic example compares a 10 MW standard connection delayed 24 months with a flexible connection delayed six months, using daily intervals and a 48-hour restriction. Daily resolution is for illustrating finance; it cannot validate sub-day response. The annual profile, cost assumptions, contribution, emissions and delays are hypothetical. For a real study use measured demand, appropriate contract and tariff resolution, site economics and operator-approved constraints.

Limitations: no inferred revenue or connection timing, no financing/tax model, inflation, degradation, time-varying annual operating years, stochastic reliability or utility-specific demand ratchets, time-of-use demand fees or coincident peaks. Residual value must be supplied explicitly. By default partial-year costs are prorated; the opt-in calendar model reoptimizes the initial partial year but does not reoptimize inter-year transitions. The engine assumes full-year perfect-foresight dispatch; separately validate causal feasibility and resource performance before investment or connection decisions.

## Repeated-year boundary check

When the same annual trace is used in more than one service year, or a seasonal commissioning trace is followed by a full year, check the relevant terminal state against the next trace’s declared initial state. The result exposes `repeat_boundary` per trace and `boundary_validation` per alternative. Battery energy, zone temperature, generator output and online state, and workload backlog must match within an absolute tolerance of 1e-5 in their respective units. Generator remaining minimum on/off dwell cannot exceed the declared initial obligation (rounded to intervals). Fuel is assumed replenished each year. A mismatch makes the alternative ineligible and blocks a complete comparison if it is otherwise feasible. This is a conservative certificate for repeating the existing traces, not proof that no continuous multi-year schedule is feasible; no state reset, reoptimization, or implicit recharge is authorized. Nonseasonal fractional years remain a prorated annual proxy. Site commissioning state and fuel replenishment must be independently confirmed.
